# What the published explainers leave out

A survey of the public secondary literature on regionalization (umc.org / Ask The UMC
parts 1–5, UM News, ResourceUMC, the Connectional Table, Good News / Lambrecht, IRD /
Juicy Ecumenism, umchurchlaw.com), compared against the ratified text. This is the
record of *why* the editorial layer says what it says. Re-run it if the site's scope
changes.

## The headline finding

**Published sources overwhelmingly cite ballot / 2016 numbering, and not one of them
tells the reader so.** A United Methodist who reads an explainer citing "¶31.5," then
opens a 2024 Book of Discipline to check it, lands on the wrong paragraph and gets no
warning from any source that this will happen.

Three converging signals put the official layer in the ballot system:

1. Ask The UMC part 5 self-identifies its edition, citing ¶101 "(2016 Book of
   Discipline)" — the pre-2019 edition whose numbering the ballots inherited.
2. The central-conference adaptation article is cited as ¶31.5 by Ask The UMC and
   ResourceUMC. In the 2024 Discipline that provision is ¶32.5.
3. **The clean discriminator:** the Ask The UMC amendments page cites Ballot 2 as ¶4 and
   Ballot 4 as ¶35. ¶4 sits *before* the 2019 insertion of ¶6, so it is unshifted and
   gives no signal. ¶35 sits *after* it. A source using 2024 numbering would have
   written ¶36. It wrote ¶35.

`umchurchlaw.com` is the only source citing numbers with real granularity, and it
interleaves both systems without labelling either — presenting one set as "2020/2024
Book of Discipline" and another as Petition 21039 provisions, which are ballot numbers.
It is simultaneously the most useful public source and the most likely to mislead.

Most official pages cite **no paragraph numbers at all**, for an amendment touching
more than twenty of them.

## Claims checked against the text

**Verified true.** "Only the regional conferences, not the jurisdictional or annual
conferences within them, would have authority to authorize adaptation" (Ask The UMC
part 4) — ¶32.5 grants adaptation to regional conferences; ¶28.5 gives jurisdictions
only administrative rule-making, expressly subject to regional conferences.

**Verified true.** Regions may set their own standards for whom to ordain and whom to
marry (UM News) — ¶32.5(a) reaches clergy qualifications, ¶32.5(d) reaches the ritual
"including ecclesial acts of marriage and burial," subject to the first and second
Restrictive Rules.

**Verified true, but mis-framed by both sides.** Lambrecht's two structural asymmetry
claims are textually correct: General Conference may redraw regional boundaries without
consent but jurisdictional boundaries only with annual-conference consent (¶17.12,
¶29.2); and jurisdiction-elected bishops hold life tenure while regions without
jurisdictions set their own (¶51).

What the critique omits, and what the official material also omits, is that **both
asymmetries pre-date the amendment.** The old ¶39 let General Conference change central
conference boundaries with no consent; the old ¶40 imposed the consent rule on
jurisdictions; the old ¶51 already split tenure the same way. The amendment carried all
three forward unchanged. So the asymmetries are real and the "equal footing" framing is
incomplete — but they are preserved, not created, and "new colonialism" overstates the
novelty. `/open` says exactly this.

**Unresolvable from the text.** Published counts of the regions disagree — UM News says
eight plus the US, umc.org's hub says nine, Ask The UMC part 4 says eight *including*
the US. The text fixes no number: ¶29.1(b) simply inherits whatever central conferences
existed before the postponed 2020 General Conference. `/p/29` says so rather than
picking a number.
